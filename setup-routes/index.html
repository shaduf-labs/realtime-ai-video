Decision guide · checked 14 September 2026

Choose the route, then test the claim

A model name does not tell you whether you are buying an interactive session, operating a clip queue, or rendering a short file locally.

Route filters

Pick Director when

You need the product shape of an open session. The current provider page says it streams through WebRTC and carries context into later directions. Plan for a finite session from the first sketch, and price $4.80 for even the stated 60-second minimum.

Pick buffered FastH3 when

You want a sequence of distinct scenes and can prebuild it. The inspected contract says a roughly 15-second clip takes about 15.5 seconds to build on four B200s and 12.9 on eight. Its streaming client enables autoplay and filler, but that is a delivery policy, not story continuity.

Pick local H3 when

You want to learn, iterate, or make short clips on your own hardware. MiniMax documents 4–15 second output. A community author reports a 5090 and 64 GB RAM making roughly 15 seconds at 768×1024 in about 22 minutes. Treat that as a condition card for an offline experiment, not a hardware promise.

Baseline card before you spend a weekend

  1. Write the model variant, task mode, and every model file or precision choice.
  2. Record GPU count, VRAM, system RAM, storage path, OS, ComfyUI version, runtime, and launch flags.
  3. Keep resolution, frames, steps, seed, sampler, and prompt fixed for a first comparison.
  4. For a queue, record cold time, warm time, manual or autoplay policy, filler, retry count, ready seconds, and black or silent fallback.
  5. Label the result as provider documentation, community report, or a measured test. Do not blend them.

Sources: MiniMax H3 repository, fal Director documentation, Reactor FastH3 README, Reactor streaming-client README, and a 5090 community report.
