Cost and throughput · checked 15 September 2026

A single 5090 channel is a playback-rate decision before it is a speed claim.

Put a clip’s source-frame duration beside build time and delivered fps, then add queue margin, retries, idle hardware, encoding, delivery, and session resets before calling a route fast or cheap.

New consumer route: source-stated 22.1 fps, named conditions

Community project says: its FastH3 Live v1.2.0 route sustains 22.1 fps on one RTX 5090 (32 GB) under Windows 11 with ComfyUI. Its named setup uses a dense four-step conversion, a 448×448 canvas, a prequeue, retimed playout, and a 721-scene library.

The source also says two prompts stay queued so the GPU is not idle. A sustained channel needs work already in flight. It does not give a new viewer a zero-wait response.

FastH3 local: published timing, not a live claim

Project says: FastVideo compared the same 832×480, 124-frame, four-step FastH3 clip at 24 fps on an M4 Max, one DGX Spark, and two DGX Sparks. A 124-frame clip is about 5.17 seconds of output. The table converts its stated times into a decision number.

FastH3’s high-end profile still has two decision rows

Project says: the current Reactor FastH3 README says a roughly 15-second clip builds in about 15.5 seconds on four B200 GPUs and 12.9 seconds on eight. Four is slightly behind the stated clip duration. Eight is ahead by the source’s own numbers. Neither line includes an independent queue-drain, retry, or cost result.

Current priced route: H3 Max Director

Provider says: the current fal page lists $0.08 per generated second, a 60-second minimum, and public sessions up to 15 minutes. That makes the stated minimum $4.80 and a stated full 15-minute session $72 in generated-video spend.

Unknown: actual billed time, direction-to-change latency, delivery cost, moderation cost, and whether an audience sees a clean restart. This arithmetic is not an operating quote.

Use this record

Native-motion fpsThe rate the source frames were authored to represent. A lower delivered fps can extend playout time while slowing motion.
Generated-minute costProvider bill or compute rate divided by usable generated seconds.
Delivered-hour costGenerated work plus idle time, retries, buffering, encoding, transport, and sessions that fail before a viewer sees value.
Ready secondsFinished footage waiting ahead of the viewer. This is the margin that protects a queue-based stream.

Sources: FastH3 Live community project, FastH3 local timing report, Reactor FastH3 README, and fal H3 Max Director.
