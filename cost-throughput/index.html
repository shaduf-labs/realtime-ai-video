Cost and throughput · checked 13 September 2026

“Runs locally” can still mean 23 seconds of work per output second

Put output duration beside wall-clock duration before calling a route fast. Then add buffer, retries, idle hardware, encoding, delivery, and session resets before calling it cheap.

FastH3 local: published timing, not a live claim

Project says: FastVideo compared the same 832×480, 124-frame, four-step FastH3 clip at 24 fps on an M4 Max, one DGX Spark, and two DGX Sparks. A 124-frame clip is about 5.17 seconds of output. The table converts its stated times into a decision number.

The calculation is elapsed time divided by 124 frames ÷ 24 fps. It does not include hardware cost, power, storage, retries, process startup, delivery, or an independent quality review. A local process that produces one clip is not a viewer-speed generator.

Queue capacity also needs a policy

Project says: Reactor reports a warm 14.4-second build for a 14.375-second FastH3 clip on four B200 GPUs. Its queue contract makes autoplay optional and off by default; a finished or stopped clip clears to black. A ready queue is only useful after you name who advances it and what happens after the last ready clip.

That is a design clue, not a rental quote or proof that another deployment has the same behavior. The clips still have hard boundaries even when a queue stays full.

Current priced route: H3 Max Director

Provider says: the current fal page lists $0.02 per generated second right now and $0.08 per second from 14 September 2026. The minimum is 60 seconds: $1.20 during the promotion and $4.80 at the stated list price. A full 15-minute public session would be $18 during the promotion or $72 at list price.

Unknown: actual billed time, direction-to-change latency, delivery cost, moderation cost, and whether an audience sees a clean restart. This arithmetic is generated-video spend, not an operating quote.

Use this record

Generated-minute costProvider bill or compute rate divided by usable generated seconds.
Delivered-hour costGenerated work plus idle time, retries, buffering, encoding, transport, and sessions that fail before a viewer sees value.
Time to readyFrom accepted input to a playable clip or active stream, including queue wait.
Ready secondsFinished footage waiting ahead of the viewer. This is the margin that protects a queue-based stream.

Sources: FastH3 local timing report, Reactor FastH3 README, and fal H3 Max Director.
