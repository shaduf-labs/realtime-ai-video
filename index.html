Checked 11 September 2026 · Research update 002

FastH3 got local. It did not get live.

The new fact: FastVideo now publishes local FastH3 Preview routes for Apple Silicon and NVIDIA DGX Spark. Its fastest stated local preview still takes about 23 wall-clock seconds to make one second of video.

The local speed check

Project says: FastVideo measured one 832×480, 124-frame, four-step FastH3 clip on an M4 Max and on one or two DGX Sparks. At 24 fps, that clip is about 5.17 seconds long. Its fastest local result—two Sparks using the TAEH3 preview decoder—took 119 seconds. That is about 23 wall-clock seconds for one second of output.

The full H3 VAE is the source’s quality path. On the same two-Spark comparison, it took 209 seconds, or about 40 wall seconds per output second. TAEH3 is a prompt-check shortcut; the source says it softens fine detail. Neither source result is a live-stream rate.

The hosted route still has the clearest live shape

Provider says: the current H3 Max Director page lists $0.02 per generated second through 14 September 2026, then $0.08 per second, with a 60-second minimum. It says public sessions run up to 15 minutes. A continuous experience is still a session-and-handoff problem, not a one-click 24/7 stream.

Unknown: the endpoint page conflicts with a fal learning article that describes a two-minute default. The pool has not measured connection time, direction delay, bill, or a restart. Recheck before paying or building around a limit.

Choose your next move

  • I want viewers to change the scene: compare Director’s session behavior with your required interaction delay and handoff plan.
  • I want a local H3 or FastH3 experiment: record hardware, system RAM, model files, runtime version, resolution, frames, steps, cold time, repeat time, quality, and failure.
  • I want a continuous-looking broadcast: design the queue, ready buffer, playout, fallback, and encoder before chasing a model benchmark.
